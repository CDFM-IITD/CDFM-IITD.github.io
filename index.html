Computational Design of Functional Materials

Welcome to the Computational Design of Functional Materials (CDFM) group at the Indian Institute of Technology Delhi. We are part of the Department of Materials Science and Engineering and the Department of Chemistry (joint) at IIT Delhi, led by Prof. Dibyajyoti Ghosh.

Efficient functional materials are key to smart, cost-effective, and green technologies. Finding the right material for a particular application is challenging and needs expertise from many fields. Atomistic understanding–guided materials design, followed by experimental realization at the device scale, is a powerful strategy to push these technologies forward.

Our group uses high-level computational simulations to investigate and manipulate energy conversion and transport processes in optoelectronic, photocatalytic, and spintronic materials. Building on this fundamental knowledge, we apply statistical and machine learning models for the in-silico design and discovery of efficient, environment-friendly materials for targeted applications.

Broad Topics of Research

Emerging Photoactive Materials for Optoelectronics and Photocatalysis

Simulating charge-carrier transport, recombination, and hot-carrier cooling in halide perovskites, kesterites, and other emerging photoactive materials, and the interfacial charge dynamics that drive photocatalysis.

Read more

High-throughput and Machine Learning for Functional Materials Design

High-throughput screening and supervised machine learning models that uncover structure–property relationships and accelerate the discovery of functional materials.

Read more

Optical Properties of Inorganic Nanocrystals for LEDs and Quantum Photonics

Surfaces, ligands, doping, and electron–phonon interactions in inorganic nanocrystals for light-emitting devices and single-photon emitters.

Read more

Low-dimensional Layered Materials for Spintronics and Nanoelectronics

Tuning magnetic interactions in low-dimensional layered materials and metal–organic frameworks with strain, electric fields, temperature, and nanostructuring.
